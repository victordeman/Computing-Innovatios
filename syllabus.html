Course Syllabus

Course Objectives

  • Explain key concepts, frameworks, and processes of innovation in computing.
  • Evaluate emerging technologies for feasibility, impact, and disruptive potential.
  • Analyze historical and current case studies of computing innovations.
  • Propose, prototype (at a high level), or critique new technology applications.
  • Discuss ethical, legal, economic, and societal implications of new computing technologies.

Assessment

Component Weight
Class participation & reading responses 15–20%
Midterm exam 20%
Group project 30–35%
Final exam or individual report 25–30%

Group Project Details

Teams of 3–4 students select an emerging technology or innovation idea. The project is a core part of the course, allowing students to apply theoretical frameworks to real-world (or future) scenarios.

Deliverables:

  1. 10–15 page analysis/report: Deep dive into the technology, market fit, and impact.
  2. 10-minute presentation + Q&A: Pitching the innovation to the class.
  3. Optional high-level prototype/mockup: Using no-code tools, Python demo, or Figma.

Example Topics: "Generative AI for personalized education in low-resource settings" or "Quantum-inspired optimization for supply-chain logistics."

Prerequisites

Intermediate programming skills and a basic understanding of computer systems are required. Students should be comfortable reading technical papers and participating in critical discussions.
